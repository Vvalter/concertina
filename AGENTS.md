# Agent notes: Note Listener (concertina)

A single-page web app that listens to an instrument through the microphone and shows:
- the note being played (letter name, Helmholtz `c'` style or scientific `C4` style) and how far it is off in cents,
- chords (name, e.g. `Am`, `G7`, `C/E`, and the individual notes),
- the notes on a grand staff (treble + bass clef),
- which button(s) to press on a **Maccann duet concertina**.

The owner plays a Maccann duet concertina and is German-speaking (hence the Helmholtz notation: c' = "eingestrichenes c").

## Layout

No build step, no dependencies, no npm packages. The only external resource is the Noto Music font from Google Fonts (clefs and ♯/♭ glyphs); it falls back to local fonts.

- `index.html`: the start menu (the app's `start_url`): one tile per page (Hören & Üben → listen.html, Hänschen klein, Duett, Guter Mond) and the version label.
- `listen.html`: the Note Listener (inline CSS and one inline `<script>` IIFE, sections below). It was index.html up to V23.
- `song.html`: plays "Hänschen klein" from the recordings, highlights each note in the score (with lyrics) and its right-hand button, and shows the next button dashed. Tempo slider (remembered), click a note to start there, space bar plays/stops. The song is the `SONG.notes` string (`<note><octave>:<quarters>:<syllable>`, bars split by `|`); more songs can use the same format. Uses the ♯/♭ and c'/C4 choice saved by the main page.
- `duet.html`: "Hänschen klein – Duett", an arrangement for duet concertina. The melody is an octave up in the right hand, the left hand plays an oom-pah bass (C/G7). The middle part is soft, in thirds over held chords, then a crescendo; the last verse is loud with a ritardando. The final note and the ending chord use a bellows shake (7 Hz tremolo). It is drawn on a grand staff (R treble, L bass); both hands' buttons light up, the next ones dashed. The arrangement is the `BARS` table (one line per bar per hand; `+` for chords, `r` for rests), plus `LEVEL` (bellows level per bar), `SLOW` (ritardando) and `SHAKE` per bar. Both hands play through one "bellows" gain: a swell over each two-bar phrase and a short dip at each bellows reversal. Chord letters (`HARMONY`, per bar, `"C G"` = G from beat 3; just C and G, G also for G7) stand above the treble staff where the harmony changes and at line starts; the current one lights up while playing. A switch picks the version: "Mit Doppelgriffen" (`BARS`) or "Einfach" (`SIMPLE`: one note per hand at a time, right hand the melody, left hand root and fifth in half notes; same bar count, so `LEVEL`/`SLOW`/`SHAKE`/`HARMONY` apply to both). Remembered in `noteListener.duetVersion`.
- `backing.html`: "Guter Mond, du gehst so stille – vierstimmiger Choral", C major, the melody starts on g' (as on the owner's reference sheet: right hand exactly the melody, the accompaniment below). Four-part chorale: soprano = melody (`SOPRANO`, with lyrics), and a three-part accompaniment, alto/tenor/bass, per bar in `GRIFFIG`, voiced for the left hand's grips on the Edeophone (the owner chose this as the only version in V24; the earlier "Schlicht" and "Mit Durchgängen" versions are in git history before V24). `GRIFFIG` is generated by `node tools/grips.mjs` (dynamic programming over the beats; cost: two buttons in one column with a button between them, more than four columns, more than two rows of vertical stretch, button movement; hard rules: ranges, voice order, no parallels, the third present, 7th chords keep the 7th) and pasted in; the tool also prints grip statistics. Re-run it after changing `HARMONY` or `SOPRANO`. In `GRIFFIG` the alto may go down to G3 (left hand). Ranges (written = sounding): S C4–A4, A B3–F4, T E3–C4, B C3–G3 (the left hand's lowest button is C3). "Links einstimmig" (default, settings key `hand` = `one`) leaves out alto and tenor: the left hand plays only the bass. "Links zweistimmig" (`two`) uses `ZWEI` (per bar [upper line, bass], generated by `VOICES=2 node tools/grips.mjs`, the third in one of the three voices). "Links dreistimmig" puts A, T and B in the left hand; "Alt rechts" gives the alto to the right hand where it has the button (B3 isn't on the right hand, so that note stays left). Each staff shows one hand; on the bottom staff alto and tenor stem up, bass down. "Es klingt" checkboxes pick the audible voices (default: not the soprano, which the player plays); they work while playing (one gain per voice). `HARMONY`: chord symbols, one per beat (`-` = none) or one per bar. No intro: bar 0 is the pickup "Gu-ter" (beat 4) with the accompaniment resting (`HARMONY` '-'; the bar's rests aren't drawn), the accompaniment starts on "Mond". Playback starts at `START` (beat 3). "Next »" (`[data-next]`, also ArrowRight) jumps to the next onset with a note; while playing it continues from there. Tempo 0 bpm is step mode: Play, Next and tapping a note sound the grip at that onset and hold it (looped samples) until the next step, notes that continue keep sounding; Stop releases them. The other pages' tempo sliders start at 1 bpm. On phones Play and Next sit together at the end of the "now" line (`.mini-btns`). The song pages have no explanatory paragraph (the owner asked for them to go in V24). Settings in `noteListener.choral.*`. After changing the voices run `node tests/voice-leading.mjs backing.html` (bar lengths, ranges, voice order, parallel fifths/octaves). Melody source: liederkiste.com (Volksweise, transposed from G); the harmonisation is our own.
- Printing: the song pages have a "🖨 Drucken" button (`[data-print="<system height>"]`: 118 song, 219 duet, 270 backing — the drawScore system height; change both together). `Concertina.printScore` redraws the score wide (sets the SVG to 900 px and fires `resize`, which every page's resize handler answers), clones each system into its own SVG in a `.print-sheet` under the title, and calls `window.print()` (phones: print dialog / save as PDF). Print CSS in concertina.css shows only that sheet, black on white.
- Keyboard diagram: `buildMaccann` draws the buttons at r 33.5 (the nearest buttons are 69.7 apart, so they almost touch) with 24 px bold labels, and sets the viewBox to the buttons' extent (`ZOOM_VIEW.both`). Tap-to-zoom on the song pages (`svg.maccann[data-zoom]`, `enableZoom`): a tap on the keyboard turns its card into a full-screen layer (`.card.zoomed`) showing only the tapped hand's side (`ZOOM_VIEW.left/right`), as big as the screen allows; the lit buttons stay live and the "now" line with Play (and Next) stays on top. A tap, ✕ or Esc goes back. On a short landscape window the zoomed side can come out smaller than the normal view; it is meant for phones in portrait.
- Phones (≤ 560 px): on listen.html the header is one row (title, ⚙, Start; the settings and song links fold out under ⚙), the letter and the staff sit side by side, and the button diagram follows directly, so all of it fits on one screen. On the song pages the keyboard bar sticks to the bottom and has its own Play/Stop button (`[data-play]`, kept in sync); on larger screens the header with Play/Stop sticks to the top instead.
- `concertina.js` (`window.Concertina`): shared by both pages. Contains note naming (`spell`, `label`), the Edeophone layout (`MACCANN`, `buildMaccann(svg)`), and the recordings (`SAMPLE_FILES`, `sampleFor`, `makeLoop`, `loadLoop(ctx, file)`, `loopCache(ctx)`, `playNote(...)`).
- `concertina.css`: shared colours, layout basics, controls and the button-diagram styles.
- `samples/`: one recording per button (see Testing for the names). Play mode and the song page need them deployed too.
- `manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js`, `pwa.js`, `icon.svg`, `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png`: the installable app (PWA), see below.

All pages load the shared files as `concertina.js?v=V24` / `concertina.css?v=V24` / `pwa.js?v=V24`. **Update that query together with the version label**, so GitHub Pages' cache can't pair a new page with old shared files. Bump `CACHE` in `sw.js` at the same time.

`listen.html` script sections, in order (search for `// ----------`):

| Section | What it does |
|---|---|
| note naming | `spell(midi, flats)` gives the letter, accidental, octave and Helmholtz name. `label(sp, helm)` picks the display style. |
| pitch detection | `detectPitch(buf, sr)`: McLeod Pitch Method (NSDF) on a 2048-sample time buffer. Returns `{rms, freq, clarity}`. |
| polyphonic detection | `detectNotes(dbSpectrum, sr, fftSize, a4)`: peak-picks a 16384-point FFT, assigns peaks to the nearest note (±35 cents), then walks upward subtracting each peak's expected overtones (`OVERTONES` table). Notes on a 2nd–10th harmonic of a lower peak are flagged `overtone`. `findChord(notes)` needs at least two pitch classes from unflagged notes and only uses flagged octaves/fifths to complete a chord. `identifyChord(midis)` template-matches against `CHORD_TYPES`. |
| staff rendering | `drawStaff(midis, flats)` draws the grand staff as SVG. Notes ≥ middle C go on treble, lower ones on bass. It handles ledger lines, second-interval head offsets, stacked accidentals, and 8va for ≥ C7. |
| Maccann keyboard | `MACCANN` holds the button positions (Edeophone 55-key). `drawMaccann(midis)` highlights buttons and writes the L/R legend. |
| UI state | `current` is `{type:'note', midi}` or `{type:'chord', chord, midis}`. `render(sound)` draws everything. The note-history row is also here. |
| chord practice | "Akkord üben" dropdown (`PRACTICE_CHORDS`, German names; groups Dur, Moll, Septakkorde, Jazz, Blues). Each entry names a kind in `PRACTICE_KINDS`: required intervals `iv`, optional ones `opt` (a 9th chord's 5th: may sound, isn't required) and the German description. `practice.pcs` = required pitch classes, `practice.allowed` = plus optional; "wrong" means not in `allowed`. While practising, the top (`drawPracticeTop`, called from `render` and `drawPractice`) shows the chord name and description, its tones in German (`practicePc`: H, B = B♭, Fis, Es …) and the grip on the staff; heard grip notes turn green, other heard notes are added green/red (`drawStaff(midis, flats, cls)`). "▶ Anhören (halten)" sounds the grip of both hands from the recordings while held (built up from the lowest note, one more every `CHORD_STEP` = 0.35 s) (pointer capture; Space/Enter too); meanwhile and for 0.4 s after, the mic loop ignores input (`previewing()`), so the speaker can't make a verdict. `startPractice` outlines a close root-position grip per hand (left from octave 3, right from octave 4); `drawMaccann` defers to `drawPractice` while practising. `setPracticeHeard` turns heard buttons green/red and writes the German verdict; while notes only drop out the verdict stays. Exit with × Beenden, "– aus –" or Esc. |
| settings | `saveSettings()` stores ♯/♭, c'/C4, chords on/off, A4, practice chord, mode and diagnostics in `localStorage` (`noteListener.settings`); restored at the end of the script. |
| audio | `getUserMedia` (echo cancellation, noise suppression and AGC all **off**; they wreck instrument audio). Two `AnalyserNode`s: 2048 for the monophonic path, 16384 for chords. The AudioContext is created before the `await` (iOS only runs one made during the tap). Phones pause the mic on screen lock, in the background or when another app takes it: `micLive()` gates the loop (a suspended analyser repeats its last buffer, which kept showing the old note), `showMicState` says "Microphone paused. Tap anywhere to continue.", any tap resumes the context, an ended track (or one still muted 1.5 s after returning) gets a fresh stream via `restart()`. A screen wake lock is held while listening. |
| play mode | "Microphone / Play buttons" switch. Tapping a diagram button toggles a looped recording from `samples/` (`SAMPLE_FILES`; buttons without one borrow the other hand's or a re-pitched neighbour). `makeLoop` (concertina.js) builds a ~3 s loop per note: from the longest steady stretch (loudness flattened) it chains 0.15–0.3 s pieces taken from random places, each joined where the waveform matches best (60 ms crossfade), so no short pattern repeats. About 30 ms per note; 50 loops are ~26 MB of audio. Needs http(s): browsers block `fetch` on `file://`. |
| diagnostics | The "Diagnostics" checkbox in the settings (remembered): a panel under the status line with frames/s, sample rate, level and peak (clipped frames), MPM clarity, why the current frame shows nothing (too quiet / unclear / chord), the confirm candidate, and the track's real `echoCancellation`/`noiseSuppression`/`autoGainControl` settings. "Record 10 s" records the mic with `MediaRecorder`; then a Save link (blob download) and, if `navigator.canShare`, a Share button appear. The file type must be bare (`audio/webm` → .webm, preferred; `audio/mp4` → .m4a, for Safari): with `;codecs=…` phones refuse to share it, and Chrome refuses `audio/mp4` (NotAllowedError, not on its share list), so a recording from a phone can be replayed in the tests. |
| loop | Per animation frame: gate on RMS. If chords are on (`useChords`, the "Notes + chords / Notes only" switch) and MPM clarity < `CHORD_CLARITY_MAX`, try chord detection first; otherwise fall back to a single note. A result must repeat for `NOTE_CONFIRM` / `CHORD_CONFIRM` frames before it is shown. |

The version label (`#version`, currently `V24`) is in the `<h1>` of index.html (the menu) and listen.html; bump both. **Bump it with every change that gets pushed.** It shows the owner which version is loaded, and it drives the update check: on load, and when the tab becomes visible again (at most once a minute), the page re-fetches itself with `fetch(location.href, {cache: 'reload'})`. That bypasses GitHub Pages' 10-minute `max-age` and refreshes the browser cache. If the fetched `#version` differs, it shows a "new version available – Reload" bar. It is skipped on `file://`.

Test hooks at the bottom of the script: `window.__detectPitch`, `window.__show(sound)`, `window.__identifyChord` and `window.__mic()` (`{ctx, stream}`).

## Installable app (PWA)

The pages can be installed on a phone ("Add to Home screen" / "Install app") and work offline. Every page has the manifest, theme-color, icon and apple-touch-icon tags in `<head>`, and loads `pwa.js` right after `concertina.js`. All paths are relative (the app lives at https://vvalter.github.io/concertina/, scope `./`).

- `manifest.webmanifest`: name, `start_url` `./index.html` (the menu), standalone, colours, icons (192, 512, the 512 again as `maskable`, and the SVG).
- `icon.svg`: a 12-sided concertina end like the owner's Edeophone (end plate, fretwork ring, 18 buttons in staggered Maccann columns, hand strap), white on the accent blue `#2f6fdb`, kept inside the central 80 % so it also works as a maskable icon. `icon-192.png` / `icon-512.png` are screenshots of it from headless Chrome (an HTML wrapper with the SVG at 100 %, `--screenshot --window-size=N,N`). Re-render them if the SVG changes.
- `pwa.js`: registers `sw.js` on http(s) only (not `file://`).
- `sw.js`, caching:
  - The shell (index (menu), listen, song, duet and backing pages, `concertina.js/.css`, `pwa.js`, manifest, icons) is precached on install. Same-origin requests are **network first**, and the cache is only an offline fallback, so a pushed version is never stuck behind the cache. Normal requests are revalidated (`cache: 'no-cache'`); requests that already ask for `cache: 'reload'` (listen.html's update check) keep that mode. Cache keys drop the query, so `?v=V17` matches the precached file (`ignoreSearch`), but the network always gets the exact URL. An offline navigation to `./` falls back to `index.html`.
  - `samples/*.m4a` and Google Fonts (`fonts.googleapis.com`, `fonts.gstatic.com`) are **cache first**, stored when first used. A song works offline once it has been played online.
  - `skipWaiting` + `clients.claim`; on activate, every `concertina-*` cache except the current one is deleted.
- **`CACHE` in `sw.js` (`concertina-V24`) must be bumped together with the version label and the `?v=` query.** Changing `sw.js` is what makes browsers install the new worker and drop the old cache.
- New pages or shared files belong in `SHELL` in `sw.js` (and the pages need the head tags and `pwa.js`).
- Tests: the pages register the worker under `tests/run.mjs` too. The song case "Hänschen klein" is timing-sensitive: note 0 stays highlighted while the recordings load, and the test records it if loading takes longer than one 40 ms poll. It flakes under load with or without the worker (seen 2026-11-21).

## Maccann layout: source and caveats

- The keyboard is the owner's own instrument: a **Lachenal Edeophone, 55 keys, Nr. 2570** (Maccann system). There are 25 buttons on the left and 30 on the right, plus an air button.
  Source: `Lachenal_Edeophone_55key_Nr2570-3.pdf` in this repo. `MACCANN` holds the PDF's label-centre coordinates (points), extracted with `pdftotext -bbox`. `buildMaccann` scales them with `X = (x-20)*1.25` and `Y = (y-200)*1.25+60`.
- Octave marks in the chart: none = 4, ¹ = 5, ² = 6, ⁻¹ = 3. That gives a left hand of C3–C5 and a right hand of C4–F6. **Still unverified on the real instrument.** If it's off by an octave, shift every note in `MACCANN`.
- The diagram bottom is assumed to be toward the hand rest (as in Gaskins' Maccann charts).
- Background on Maccann layouts in general: Robert Gaskins, *How to Play Chords on Any MacCann Duet Concertina* (2001),
  http://www.concertina.com/gaskins/chords/Gaskins-How-to-Play-Chords-on-Any-MacCann-Duet-Concertina-3.pdf (use plain http).

## Tuning knobs (chord / note detection)

- `OVERTONES` (semitone offset and max overtone:fundamental ratio). Higher ratios suppress more spurious notes but also swallow real notes that coincide with overtones, e.g. right hand C5/E5/G5 over left hand C3. The current values were chosen by testing two synthetic timbres (reedy: 2nd harmonic 2× the fundamental; balanced). Measured on the owner's Edeophone (`samples/`), harmonics are often 1–5× the fundamental and up to 13× (left C5). So the ratios can't separate overtones from notes; `HARMONICS` / `findChord` do that instead.
- Note thresholds inside `detectNotes`: a peak must be ≥ 12 % of the loudest peak to count as a note; peaks down to 3 % still subtract overtones.
- `CHORD_CONFIRM = 6`: when a single note is released, up to 3 frames can look like a chord.
- `CHORD_CLARITY_MAX = 0.97`: single notes have MPM clarity ≈ 1.0, chords 0.4–0.93.
- `RMS_GATE`, `CLARITY_GATE`, `*_CONFIRM` and `SILENCE_FRAMES` live in the detection-smoothing block.

## Testing

`node tests/run.mjs` (about a minute; `-j N` sets parallel Chrome instances, default 6; extra words filter cases by group or name, e.g. `node tests/run.mjs chord practice`). It serves the repo, opens the unmodified `listen.html` in headless Chrome, and plays recordings from `samples/` into it as a fake microphone (several at once for chords). Groups:
- `single`: each of the 50 recordings must show only its own note.
- `chord`: 23 chords and intervals mixed from recordings must show the right chord, intervals no chord. One known failure: F3 + F4/A4/C5 (the C5 reed's weak fundamental is subtracted as F3's 3rd harmonic).
- `notes-only`: with chord detection off, no chord is shown.
- `practice`, `practice-single`: practice-mode verdicts and green/red buttons, incl. jazz/blues chords (9th without its 5th, German tone names). `staff and Anhören`: the grip on the staff, a heard note green, and the Anhören button sounds only while held.
- `mic`: a suspended AudioContext shows "paused" and stops showing the old note, a tap resumes it; an ended track gets a new stream. Notes are recognised after both.
- `play`: tapped buttons sound at the right pitch until tapped again; switching to Microphone stops them.
- `song`: song.html highlights the notes in order, the right button is lit, and the right pitch sounds.
- `song` (Guter Mond – Choral): backing.html in C major shows only C-major chord symbols (≥ 40) and no key signature; by default the left hand lights only the bass and the tempo slider starts at 0 bpm; with "Links dreistimmig", from "dir nicht folgen kann" the right hand lights only the melody and the left hand the grips C3 E3 G3 (under E4, then under C4), something sounds, playback stops at the end, and there is no Fassung switch; "Links zweistimmig" lights at most two left buttons; the score starts with "Gu- ter" and the first chord symbol is at "Mond"; at 0 bpm tapping "Gu-" then Next twice lights RG4, RF4, LC3 RE4, holds the sound and Stop silences it.
- `song` (Tastatur vergrößern): on a 390×800 phone window a tap on the keyboard's right half zooms to the right hand's side (keys > 1.3× bigger), buttons light there while playing, a second tap goes back.
- `song` (Menü): index.html has the four tiles in order, every link loads, the version label is there.
- `song` (Duett): duet.html lights both hands' buttons, the bellows-shake bar pulses at ~7 Hz, and playback stops at the end. `Duett, einfach`: never more than one button per hand, the current chord letter lights up. Both check the chord letters (C/G, ≥ 17).

Sample names: `<links|rechts>_Spalte<col>_<row>_<note><octave>` (no octave suffix = 4, `1` = 5, `2` = 6, `-1` = 3). The left hand's column 4 (D3, E♭4, D4, A4, B♭4) has no recordings. Needs Node 22+ and Google Chrome (`CHROME=` to override; on a host whose AppArmor blocks Chrome's sandbox, point it at a wrapper that adds `--no-sandbox`). Chrome runs with `--disable-audio-output` (a fake output device): without it, a broken system audio output (seen after a reboot) leaves the AudioContext clock at 0 and every case times out.

For a quick algorithm check without a browser, slice the script between `const MIN_FREQ` and `// ---------- staff rendering` and wrap it in `new Function(body + '; return {detectPitch, detectNotes, findChord, identifyChord}')`.
